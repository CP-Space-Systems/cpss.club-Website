About CPSS

Mission

Cal Poly Space Systems gives students hands-on experience in designing, building, testing, launching, and recovering rockets. Guided by Cal Poly’s “Learn by Doing” philosophy, we welcome participants from all majors and backgrounds to develop practical skills in propulsion, structures, controls, recovery, and electronics.

Heritage & Background

Based at California Polytechnic State University in San Luis Obispo, CPSS is built on student initiative, hard work, and independence. Our projects are designed, manufactured, and operated entirely by students. As these projects grow in complexity, we continue to expand our resources and capabilities to support increasingly ambitious work.

Our roots reach back to Cal Poly’s Space Program in the late 1970s. Students across departments developed Space Shuttle Get Away Special payloads, and Cal Poly Space Systems was chartered as an ASI club in 1989. That interdisciplinary, hands-on heritage continues in today’s student-led projects.

From the CPSS archives

A history of building.

  1. 1978–1979

    Space Program beginnings

    A donated Space Shuttle payload opportunity in 1978 led to the Physics Department’s Cal Poly Space Program in 1979. Students worked on self-contained experimental canisters.

  2. 1980s

    Shuttle payload development

    Students from multiple departments designed and analyzed Get Away Special experiments. The archival material includes electroplating concepts, EVA welding workstation designs and proposed long-duration biological experiments.

  3. 1989

    CPSS becomes a club

    Cal Poly Space Systems was chartered by ASI, continuing an interdisciplinary student space-program tradition.

  4. 1993

    GAS-450 flies

    The GAS-450 payload brought student experiments to orbit on STS-57, which launched June 21, 1993. The payload returned for student analysis after the mission.

  5. Late 1990s–2000s

    Toward student rocketry

    The archive records sounding-rocket and paraglider work, followed by Starbooster, Rocketoon and hybrid-propulsion projects.

  6. Today

    Learn by Doing continues

    CPSS members develop flight vehicles, propulsion systems, avionics and recovery technologies through student-led design, manufacturing and testing.
