Spring 2026: Testing, Building, and Preparing for Flight

Spring 2026 · Cal Poly Space Systems

Spring brought progress across Cal Poly Space Systems—from liquid-engine testing and avionics integration to certification launches and introductory engineering projects. Here’s what our teams worked on throughout the quarter.

Archerfish moves toward integrated testing

The Archerfish team manufactured the vehicle’s composite body tubes, thrust-chamber assembly, and propellant tanks. The thrust-chamber assembly passed hydrostatic testing at 800 psi, with cold-flow testing underway.

Pump development continued alongside the vehicle. Testing produced an experimental pump-performance curve for comparison with the team’s mathematical model, helping guide further development and integration.

More experience on the test stand

The Liquids team conducted cold-flow and hotfire tests of its 300 lbf ablative engine on the Bluegrass test stand, refining operating procedures and test documentation along the way.

At NILE, the team successfully hotfired a 35 lbf SILLE engine. The test marked an important step for both the facility and the introductory liquids program, giving students experience taking an engine from design through manufacturing and testing.

Airbrakes and avionics come together

The Airbrakes team brought up its TOM flight computer and integrated its IMU, altimeter, and GPS. Work also included LoRa telemetry testing and trials connecting the PID controller to the linear actuator using simulated sensor data.

These tests laid the groundwork for further validation before flight.

Lessons from the booster program

The Fly-back Booster team completed airframe design and manufacturing development before a spring launch attempt. A motor misfire caused a failure on the launch rail, preventing the planned glide demonstration.

The team’s next design cycle will build on that experience, with a focus on making the booster smaller, lighter, and easier to manufacture.

Building skills across the club

Approximately 15 students earned their L1 rocketry certifications, and one earned an L2 certification. Meanwhile, SILLE introduced newer members to liquid-engine design, machining, test operations, and safety through mentored projects.

CPSS members also visited Redwire’s San Luis Obispo facility and presented their work to Cal Poly’s Aerospace Industry Advisory Board.
