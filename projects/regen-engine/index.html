Regeneratively Cooled Engine

A 300 lbf nitrous oxide–ethanol engine with 10 helical regenerative cooling channels, designed to characterize small-scale engine thermal performance and validate a thermal model for future higher-performance engines.

Background & Rationale

The project aims to deliver CPSS’s first successful regeneratively cooled engine. By circulating coolant through 10 helical channels, the team is investigating the thermal performance of small-scale engines and validating its thermal model.

This work is intended to support higher-performance engines, reduce component consumption, and expand the club’s research capabilities. The resulting material and performance data will provide a foundation for future engine development.

Key Mission Goals

01
Sustain regenerative cooling throughout a hotfire, working toward the team’s “indefinite” cooling objective.
02
Validate thermal models against test data.
03
Collect material data and characterize engine performance.
04
Improve CPSS’s testing cadence.

Progress & Current Work

Completed: Engine and injector design, thermal modeling, stress calculations, integration planning, test planning, and instrumentation for data collection.

Current work: Manufacturing and design refinements.

Next: Hydrostatic tests, cold-flow tests, hotfires, and further characterization. Testing dates are still to be set.
