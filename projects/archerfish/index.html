Archerfish

A student-driven bipropellant flight vehicle aiming to demonstrate a fuel pump-fed rocket, designed for 400 lbf thrust and a target apogee of 13,000 ft.

Background & Rationale

CPSS is pursuing a collegiate pump-fed liquid rocket to give students experience developing a flight vehicle and working on a mechanical pump.

Key Mission Goals

01
Develop a fuel pump-fed flight vehicle.
02
Achieve the design target of 400 lbf thrust.
03
Successfully recover the flight vehicle.

Progress & Current Work

All subsystems have been manufactured. Liquid propellent tanks have been hydrostatic tested to design requirements and TCA is currently being coldflowed. Next steps include parachute testing, engine hot fire, and full-stack cold flow.

Fuel Pump Development

Pump lead: Anthony Gregory Casagrande

The Archerfish fuel-pump effort targets a head rise of 500 PSI, a fuel mass flow of 0.26 kg/s, and a speed of about 30,000 RPM to feed the nitrous oxide and ethanol engine.

Background & rationale

This project addresses the parallelism with industry where most commercial rockets use at least one turbopump to pump liquids into the combustion chamber with the main reason of being a more compact and weight efficient system. CPSS is pursuing this goal as a proof of concept to see that we can accomplish a task that is commonly seen in industry with liquid rocketry.

Pump goals

Achieve a head rise of 500 PSI and a post-pump mass flow of 0.26 kg/s at 30,000 RPM.

Current work

The design has been reiterated for a more efficient flow path preventing backflow and we are beginning manufacturing shortly before we begin testing to develop a pump curve. After testing to validate the pump fits requirements with the pump curve test, we will begin integrating with the test stand and rest of the engine to begin a full stack cold flow.
